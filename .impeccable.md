## Design Context

### Users

The primary audience is software architects and developers attending a 90-minute online course. They need to build a fast, durable mental model of Agentic AI patterns, distinguish levels and relationship types, and apply that vocabulary in architecture discussions without requiring framework-specific implementation detail.

### Brand Personality

Editorial, rigorous, and lucid. The experience should feel like a carefully edited technical field guide: authoritative without being ceremonial, visually distinctive without distracting from structural reasoning, and calm enough for sustained learning.

### Aesthetic Direction

Continue the existing Agentic Pattern Atlas publication style: warm paper surfaces, dark ink, serif display typography paired with a clear sans-serif body, restrained rules and annotations, and the established L1–L4 palette. The primary presentation context is a 16:9 desktop screen in an online course. Avoid generic SaaS dashboards, neon AI visuals, glassmorphism, dense card grids, and decorative technology imagery.

### Design Principles

1. Make structural relationships visible before adding terminology.
2. Give each slide one teachable claim and use progressive disclosure to control cognitive load.
3. Use L1–L4 color only when it communicates level, not as decoration.
4. Reuse the atlas's topology diagrams as primary evidence and preserve their visual grammar.
5. Optimize for live delivery: large type, strong contrast, keyboard control, and legibility after screen sharing.
